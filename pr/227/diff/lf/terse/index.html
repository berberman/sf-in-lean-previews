20+-- ## Welcome
21+
22+-- This is the entry point to a series of electronic textbooks
23+-- on various aspects of *Software Foundations*, the
24+-- mathematical underpinnings of reliable software. Topics in
25+-- the series include basic concepts of logic,
26+-- computer-assisted theorem proving, the Rocq prover,
27+-- functional programming, operational semantics, logics and
28+-- techniques for reasoning about programs, static type
29+-- systems, property-based random testing, and verification of
30+-- practical C code. The exposition is intended for a broad
31+-- range of readers, from advanced undergraduates to PhD
32+-- students and researchers. No specific background in logic or
33+-- programming languages is assumed, though a degree of
34+-- mathematical maturity will be helpful.
35+
36+-- The principal novelty of the series is that it is one
37+-- hundred percent formalized and machine-checked: each text is
38+-- literally a script for Rocq. The books are intended to be
39+-- read alongside (or inside) an interactive session with Rocq.
40+-- All the details in the text are fully formalized in Rocq,
41+-- and most of the exercises are designed to be worked using
42+-- Rocq.
43+
44+-- The files in each book are organized into a sequence of core
45+-- chapters, covering about one semester's worth of material
46+-- and organized into a coherent linear narrative, plus a
47+-- number of "offshoot" chapters covering additional topics.
48+-- All the core chapters are suitable for both upper-level
49+-- undergraduate and graduate students.
50+
51+-- This book, *Logical Foundations*, lays groundwork for the
52+-- others, introducing the reader to the basic ideas of
53+-- functional programming, constructive logic, and the Rocq
54+-- prover.

56+-- ## Overview
57+
58+-- Building reliable software is hard -- really hard. The scale
59+-- and complexity of modern systems, the number of people
60+-- involved, and the range of demands placed on them make it
61+-- challenging to build software that is even more-or-less
62+-- correct, much less 100% correct. At the same time, the
63+-- increasing degree to which information processing is woven
64+-- into every aspect of society greatly amplifies the cost of
65+-- bugs and insecurities.
66+
67+-- Computer scientists and software engineers have responded to
68+-- these challenges by developing a host of techniques for
69+-- improving software reliability, ranging from recommendations
70+-- about managing software projects teams (e.g., extreme
71+-- programming) to design philosophies for libraries (e.g.,
72+-- model-view-controller, publish-subscribe, etc.) and
73+-- programming languages (e.g., object-oriented programming,
74+-- functional programming, ...) to mathematical techniques for
75+-- specifying and reasoning about properties of software and
76+-- tools for helping validate these properties. The *Software
77+-- Foundations* series is focused on this last set of tools.
78+
79+-- This volume weaves together three conceptual threads:
80+
81+-- (A) basic tools from *logic* for making and justifying
82+-- precise claims about programs;
83+
84+-- (B) the use of *proof assistants* (or *provers*) to
85+-- construct rigorous logical arguments;
86+
87+-- (C) *functional programming*, both as a method of
88+-- programming that simplifies reasoning about programs and as
89+-- a bridge between programming and logic.
90+
91+-- ### Logic
92+
93+-- Logic is the field of study whose subject matter is *proofs*
94+-- -- unassailable arguments for the truth of particular
95+-- propositions. Volumes have been written about the central
96+-- role of logic in computer science. Manna and Waldinger
97+-- called it "the calculus of computer science," while Halpern
98+-- et al.'s paper *On the Unusual Effectiveness of Logic in
99+-- Computer Science* catalogs scores of ways in which logic
100+-- offers critical tools and insights. Indeed, they observe
101+-- that, "As a matter of fact, logic has turned out to be
102+-- significantly more effective in computer science than it has
103+-- been in mathematics. This is quite remarkable, especially
104+-- since much of the impetus for the development of logic
105+-- during the past one hundred years came from mathematics."
106+
107+-- In particular, the fundamental tools of *inductive proof*
108+-- are ubiquitous in all of computer science. You have surely
109+-- seen them before, perhaps in a course on discrete math or
110+-- analysis of algorithms, but in this course we will examine
111+-- them more deeply than you have probably done so far.

117+-- ### Proof Assistants
118+
119+-- The flow of ideas between logic and computer science has not
120+-- been unidirectional: CS has also made important
121+-- contributions to logic. One of these has been the
122+-- development of software tools for helping construct proofs
123+-- of logical propositions. These tools fall into two broad
124+-- categories:
125+
126+-- - *Automated theorem provers* provide "push-button" operation:
127+--   you give them a proposition and they return either *true* or
128+--   *false* (or, sometimes, *don't know: ran out of time*).
129+--   Although their reasoning capabilities are still limited,
130+--   they have matured tremendously in recent decades and are
131+--   used now in a multitude of settings. Examples of such tools
132+--   include SAT solvers, SMT solvers, and model checkers.
133+
134+-- - *Proof assistants* are hybrid tools that automate the more
135+--   routine aspects of building proofs while depending on human
136+--   guidance for more difficult aspects. Widely used proof
137+--   assistants include Isabelle, Agda, Twelf, ACL2, PVS, F*,
138+--   HOL4, Lean, and Rocq, among many others.
139+
140+-- This course is based around Rocq, a proof assistant that has
141+-- been under development since 1983 and has attracted a large
142+-- community of users in both research and industry. Rocq
143+-- provides a rich environment for interactive development of
144+-- machine-checked formal reasoning. The kernel of the Rocq
145+-- system is a simple proof-checker, which guarantees that only
146+-- correct deduction steps are ever performed. On top of this
147+-- kernel, the Rocq environment provides high-level facilities
148+-- for proof development, including a large library of common
149+-- definitions and lemmas, powerful tactics for constructing
150+-- complex proofs semi-automatically, and a special-purpose
151+-- programming language for defining new proof-automation
152+-- tactics for specific situations.
153+
154+-- Rocq has been a critical enabler for a huge variety of work
155+-- across computer science and mathematics:
156+
157+-- - As a *platform for modeling programming languages*, it has
158+--   become a standard tool for researchers who need to describe
159+--   and reason about complex language definitions. It has been
160+--   used, for example, to check the security of the JavaCard
161+--   platform, obtaining the highest level of common criteria
162+--   certification, and for formal specifications of the x86 and
163+--   LLVM instruction sets and programming languages such as C.
164+
165+-- - As an *environment for developing formally certified
166+--   software and hardware*, Rocq has been used, for example, to
167+--   build CompCert, a fully-verified optimizing compiler for C,
168+--   and CertiKOS, a fully verified hypervisor, for proving the
169+--   correctness of subtle algorithms involving floating point
170+--   numbers, and as the basis for CertiCrypt, FCF, and SSProve,
171+--   which are frameworks for proving cryptographic algorithms
172+--   secure. It is also being used to build verified
173+--   implementations of the open-source RISC-V processor
174+--   architecture.
175+
176+-- - As a *realistic environment for functional programming with
177+--   dependent types*, it has inspired numerous innovations. For
178+--   example, Hoare Type Theory embeds reasoning about
179+--   "pre-conditions" and "post-conditions" (an extension of the
180+--   *Hoare Logic* we will see later in this course) in Rocq.
181+
182+-- - As a *proof assistant for higher-order logic*, it has been
183+--   used to validate a number of important results in
184+--   mathematics. For example, its ability to include complex
185+--   computations inside proofs made it possible to develop the
186+--   first formally verified proof of the 4-color theorem. This
187+--   proof had previously been controversial among mathematicians
188+--   because it required checking a large number of
189+--   configurations using a program. In the Rocq formalization,
190+--   everything is checked, including the correctness of the
191+--   computational part. More recently, an even more massive
192+--   effort led to a Rocq formalization of the Feit-Thompson
193+--   Theorem, the first major step in the classification of
194+--   finite simple groups.
195+

201+-- ### Functional Programming
202+
203+-- The term *functional programming* refers both to a
204+-- collection of programming idioms that can be used in almost
205+-- any programming language and to a family of programming
206+-- languages designed to emphasize these idioms, including
207+-- Haskell, OCaml, Standard ML, F##, Scala, Scheme, Racket,
208+-- Common Lisp, Clojure, Erlang, F*, and Rocq.
209+
210+-- Functional programming has been developed over many decades
211+-- -- indeed, its roots go back to Church's lambda-calculus,
212+-- which was invented in the 1930s, well *before* the first
213+-- electronic computers! But since the early '90s it has
214+-- enjoyed a surge of interest among industrial engineers and
215+-- language designers, playing a key role in high-value systems
216+-- at companies like Jane Street Capital, Microsoft, Facebook,
217+-- Twitter, and Ericsson.
218+
219+-- The most basic tenet of functional programming is that, as
220+-- much as possible, computation should be *pure*, in the sense
221+-- that the only effect of execution should be to produce a
222+-- result: it should be free from *side effects* such as I/O,
223+-- assignments to mutable variables, redirecting pointers, etc.
224+-- For example, whereas an *imperative* sorting function might
225+-- take a list of numbers and rearrange its pointers to put the
226+-- list in order, a pure sorting function would take the
227+-- original list and return a *new* list containing the same
228+-- numbers in sorted order.
229+
230+-- A significant benefit of this style of programming is that
231+-- it makes programs easier to understand and reason about. If
232+-- every operation on a data structure yields a new data
233+-- structure, leaving the old one intact, then there is no need
234+-- to worry about how that structure is being shared and
235+-- whether a change by one part of the program might break an
236+-- invariant relied on by another part of the program. These
237+-- considerations are particularly critical in concurrent
238+-- systems, where every piece of mutable state that is shared
239+-- between threads is a potential source of pernicious bugs.
240+-- Indeed, a large part of the recent interest in functional
241+-- programming in industry is due to its simpler behavior in
242+-- the presence of concurrency.
243+
244+-- Another reason for the current excitement about functional
245+-- programming is related to the first: functional programs are
246+-- often much easier to parallelize and physically distribute
247+-- than their imperative counterparts. If running a computation
248+-- has no effect other than producing a result, then it does
249+-- not matter *where* it is run. Similarly, if a data structure
250+-- is never modified destructively, then it can be copied
251+-- freely, across cores or across the network. Indeed, the
252+-- "Map-Reduce" idiom, which lies at the heart of massively
253+-- distributed query processors like Hadoop and is used by
254+-- Google to index the entire web is a classic example of
255+-- functional programming.
256+
257+-- For purposes of this course, functional programming has yet
258+-- another significant attraction: it serves as a bridge
259+-- between logic and computer science. Indeed, Rocq itself can
260+-- be viewed as a combination of a small but extremely
261+-- expressive functional programming language plus a set of
262+-- tools for stating and proving logical assertions. Moreover,
263+-- when we come to look more closely, we find that these two
264+-- sides of Rocq are actually aspects of the very same
265+-- underlying machinery -- i.e., *proofs are programs*.
266+
267+-- ### Rocq vs. Coq
268+
269+-- Until 2025, the Rocq prover was known as Coq. According to
270+-- the official webpage, "The name 'Coq' referenced the
271+-- Calculus of Constructions (CoC), the foundational system it
272+-- is based on, as well as one of its creators, Thierry
273+-- Coquand. Additionally, it paid homage to the French national
274+-- symbol, the rooster. The new name, 'the Rocq Prover', honors
275+-- Inria Rocquencourt, the original site where the prover was
276+-- developed. It also alludes to the mythological bird Roc (or
277+-- Rokh), symbolizing strength and not so disconnected to a
278+-- rooster. Furthermore, the name conveys a sense of solidity,
279+-- and its unintended connection to music adds a pleasant
280+-- resonance."
281+
282+-- The current release of Software Foundations is still in a
283+-- transitional state, and you will see references to both Coq
284+-- and Rocq.

499+-- ### Exercises
500+
501+-- Each chapter includes numerous exercises. Each is marked
502+-- with a "star rating," which can be interpreted as follows:
503+
504+-- - One star: easy exercises that underscore points in the text
505+--   and that, for most readers, should take only a minute or
506+--   two. Get in the habit of working these as you reach them.
507+
508+-- - Two stars: straightforward exercises (five or ten minutes).
509+
510+-- - Three stars: exercises requiring a bit of thought (ten
511+--   minutes to half an hour).
512+
513+-- - Four and five stars: more difficult exercises (half an hour
514+--   and up).
515+
516+-- Those using SF in a classroom setting should note that the
517+-- autograder assigns extra points to harder exercises:
518+
519+-- 1 star  = 1 point
520+-- 2 stars = 2 points
521+-- 3 stars = 3 points
522+-- 4 stars = 6 points
523+-- 5 stars = 10 points
524+
525+-- Some exercises are marked "advanced," and some are marked
526+-- "optional." Doing just the non-optional, non-advanced
527+-- exercises should provide good coverage of the core material.
528+-- Optional exercises provide a bit of extra practice with key
529+-- concepts and introduce secondary themes that may be of
530+-- interest to some readers. Advanced exercises are for readers
531+-- who want an extra challenge and a deeper cut at the
532+-- material.
533+
534+-- **Please do not post solutions to the exercises in a public
535+-- place.** Software Foundations is widely used both for
536+-- self-study and for university courses. Having solutions
537+-- easily available makes it much less useful for courses,
538+-- which typically have graded homework assignments. We
539+-- especially request that readers not post solutions to the
540+-- exercises anyplace where they can be found by search
541+-- engines.
