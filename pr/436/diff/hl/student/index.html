5---  This volume is a work in progress. It will develop a Lean formalization
6---  of Hoare Logic, covering axiomatic semantics, program verification, and
7---  reasoning about imperative programs. Please check back later!
5+--  ## Welcome
86 
9---  ## Recommended Citation Format
7+--  This is Hoare Logic, volume 2 of *Software Foundations in Lean*. It
8+--  develops formal techniques for reasoning about what programs do. For
9+--  example, with the techniques we present, one can prove that an
10+--  algorithm sorts an array or that a compiler optimization does not
11+--  incorrectly change the behavior of the program it optimizes. This
12+--  volume complements Type Systems, which develops techniques for
13+--  establishing properties of *all* programs written in a given language;
14+--  the two volumes can be read in either order, and both build on the
15+--  material in Logical Foundations.
16+
17+--  ## Overview
18+
19+--  To reason about a program, we first need a way of representing it as a
20+--  mathematical object, so that we can talk about it precisely, together
21+--  with a way of describing its behavior in terms of a mathematical
22+--  function or relation. Our main tool for this is *operational semantics*
23+--  in "big step" style, which is a method of specifying the meaning of a
24+--  programming language by writing an abstract interpreter for it.
25+--
26+--  The programming language we consider throughout this volume is *Imp*, a
27+--  toy language capturing the core features of conventional imperative
28+--  programming: variables, assignment, conditionals, and loops. Imp's
29+--  arithmetic and boolean expressions are developed as their own simple
30+--  sublangage, called Slang.
31+--
32+--  We study two different ways of reasoning about the behavior of Imp
33+--  programs.
34+--
35+--  First, we consider what it means to say that two Imp programs are
36+--  *equivalent*, in the sense that they produce the same behavior when
37+--  started in the same initial state. This notion of equivalence becomes a
38+--  criterion for judging the correctness of program transformations, such
39+--  as those used in compilers and optimizers. We build some simple
40+--  optimizers for Imp and prove that they preserve the behavior of the
41+--  programs they transform.
42+--
43+--  Second, we develop a methodology for proving that a given Imp program
44+--  satisfies a formal specification of its behavior. We introduce *Hoare
45+--  triples* — Imp programs annotated with pre- and post-conditions
46+--  describing what they expect to be true of their starting state and what
47+--  they promise to be true of their ending state, if they terminate — and
48+--  the reasoning principles of *Hoare Logic*, a domain-specific logic for
49+--  compositional reasoning about imperative programs. We then develop
50+--  *decorated programs*, a practical notation for writing out Hoare Logic
51+--  proofs alongside the code they verify.
52+--
53+--  The techniques this volume presents are relatively simple, but they
54+--  nevertheless underpin many of today's real-world software and hardware
