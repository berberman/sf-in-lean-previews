7+--  This is Hoare Logic, volume 2 of *Software Foundations

9+--  about what programs do. For example, with the techniques
10+--  we present, one can prove that an algorithm sorts an
11+--  array or that a compiler optimization does not
12+--  incorrectly change the behavior of the program it
13+--  optimizes. This volume complements Type Systems, which
14+--  develops techniques for establishing properties of *all*
15+--  programs written in a given language; the two volumes
16+--  can be read in either order, and both build on the
17+--  material in Logical Foundations.
18+
19+--  ## Overview
20+
21+--  To reason about a program, we first need a way of
22+--  representing it as a mathematical object, so that we can
23+--  talk about it precisely, together with a way of
24+--  describing its behavior in terms of a mathematical
25+--  function or relation. Our main tool for this is
26+--  *operational semantics* in "big step" style, which is a
27+--  method of specifying the meaning of a programming
28+--  language by writing an abstract interpreter for it.
29+--
30+--  The programming language we consider throughout this
31+--  volume is *Imp*, a toy language capturing the core
32+--  features of conventional imperative programming:
33+--  variables, assignment, conditionals, and loops. Imp's
34+--  arithmetic and boolean expressions are developed as
35+--  their own simple sublangage, called Slang.
36+--
37+--  We study two different ways of reasoning about the
38+--  behavior of Imp programs.
39+--
40+--  First, we consider what it means to say that two Imp
41+--  programs are *equivalent*, in the sense that they
42+--  produce the same behavior when started in the same
43+--  initial state. This notion of equivalence becomes a
44+--  criterion for judging the correctness of program
45+--  transformations, such as those used in compilers and
46+--  optimizers. We build some simple optimizers for Imp and
47+--  prove that they preserve the behavior of the programs
48+--  they transform.
49+--
50+--  Second, we develop a methodology for proving that a
51+--  given Imp program satisfies a formal specification of
52+--  its behavior. We introduce *Hoare triples* — Imp
53+--  programs annotated with pre- and post-conditions
54+--  describing what they expect to be true of their starting
55+--  state and what they promise to be true of their ending
56+--  state, if they terminate — and the reasoning principles
57+--  of *Hoare Logic*, a domain-specific logic for
58+--  compositional reasoning about imperative programs. We
59+--  then develop *decorated programs*, a practical notation
60+--  for writing out Hoare Logic proofs alongside the code
61+--  they verify.
62+--
63+--  The techniques this volume presents are relatively
64+--  simple, but they nevertheless underpin many of today's
65+--  real-world software and hardware verification efforts.
